The strongest file-size lever. Images larger than this are shrunk proportionally (both sides equally, no distortion) until their longest side equals this value; smaller images are never enlarged. Halving this value roughly quarters the file size. Must be >= 1. Reference values: • 3840 (4K) = maximum detail • 2560 (QHD) = balanced • 1920 (Full HD) = default, compact files
%
Determines how sharp the image should look. Bounds: 1 to 100. • 90 = High Quality (Recommended) • 75 = Good Quality (Web Standard) • 50 = Medium Quality • 20 = Lowest acceptable (below this, our tests showed visible discoloration) • 10 = Terrible Quality (Heavy compression)
%
The quality level the app refuses to go below, even if the file then exceeds the {size}. Without this floor, the app would keep degrading an image just to hit a size number. In our testing, a floor of 10 produced visibly discolored photos, while 20 still looked good — hence the default of 20. Bounds: 1 to 100.
A soft target, not a hard limit. The app reduces image quality until the file fits under this size. If even the {quality} is not small enough, the file is saved anyway — larger than this target — and the report notes it. To make files reliably smaller, lower {resolution} instead. Must be >= 1.
